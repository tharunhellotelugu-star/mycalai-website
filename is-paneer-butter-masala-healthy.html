Nutrition Guide

Is paneer butter masala healthy?

Paneer butter masala sits in an awkward spot nutritionally: it's a genuinely good source of protein and calcium, but a restaurant serving can easily carry 400–550 calories in a single 200g portion — more than double a home-cooked version made with less butter and cream. Whether it's "healthy" mostly comes down to portion and preparation, not the dish itself.

Why the calorie range is so wide

Paneer butter masala's calorie count depends heavily on three ingredients that vary a lot between kitchens:

VersionApprox. calories (200g)Why
Home-style, light cream280–350 kcalLess butter, cream used sparingly
Standard restaurant400–480 kcalGenerous butter + cream base
Rich/premium restaurant480–550+ kcalExtra butter, cashew paste, more cream

The paneer itself is fairly consistent in calories — it's the gravy (butter, cream, and often cashew paste for thickness and sweetness) that swings the total. A dish that looks identical in a photo can differ by 150+ calories depending entirely on how much butter went into the base.

The protein and calcium numbers are legitimately good. The fat number is where moderation matters, especially if you're eating it alongside naan (which adds its own butter/ghee) or rice.

Is it healthy, then?

In moderation, yes — paneer is a solid protein source, and an occasional rich gravy dish isn't something to avoid entirely. The concerns are:

  • Portion creep. Restaurant portions are often larger than a "serving," and the dish is rich enough that it's easy to eat more than intended without feeling it until later.
  • Pairing. Butter naan + paneer butter masala + a sweet lassi is a very calorie-dense combination even though each item alone seems reasonable.
  • Frequency. As an occasional dish, it fits into most diets fine. As a 3–4x/week staple, the saturated fat load adds up.

Simple ways to make it lighter without losing the dish

  • Ask for (or cook with) less butter and swap some of the cream for milk or a small amount of cashew paste — you keep the richness and creaminess, cut real calories.
  • Pair with roti instead of butter naan — saves 100+ calories right there.
  • Watch the portion of gravy relative to paneer — spooning extra gravy over rice roughly doubles the fat you're eating for not much extra satisfaction.

Why this dish is hard for photo-based calorie trackers

Paneer butter masala is a textbook case of the "hidden fat" problem in AI food scanning: the butter and cream are fully incorporated into the gravy, not sitting visibly on top the way oil pools on a curry. A photo alone genuinely can't distinguish a light home-style version from a rich restaurant one — the paneer cubes look the same either way.

This is exactly the kind of dish where a scanning app needs to reason about typical preparation rather than only what's visible: a restaurant-style creamy gravy with a glossy surface should be estimated on the richer end of the range by default, not assumed to be light just because the fat isn't sitting in a visible puddle. MyCal AI's estimates specifically account for this — looking for gravy sheen and richness cues in photos, and defaulting to realistic restaurant-preparation norms when you log a dish by voice or text instead.

Frequently asked questions

How many calories are in paneer butter masala with naan?

A standard serving (200g paneer butter masala + 1 butter naan) typically totals 650–800 calories, with the naan alone contributing 250–320.

Is paneer butter masala good for weight loss?

Not ideal as a frequent choice due to its calorie density, but an occasional smaller portion, paired with roti instead of naan and a side salad, can fit into most weight-loss plans.

Which has fewer calories, paneer tikka masala or paneer butter masala?

Paneer tikka masala is usually somewhat lighter (350–420 kcal per 200g) since the paneer is grilled first rather than simmered directly in a butter-cream gravy, though this varies by kitchen.
