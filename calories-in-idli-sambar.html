Calories in idli sambar

A typical breakfast of 3 idlis with sambar and a small serving of coconut chutney comes to roughly 250–350 calories — genuinely one of the lighter common Indian breakfasts, mainly because idli is steamed rather than fried or pan-cooked in oil.

Breaking down the plate

ComponentTypical amountCalories
Idli (steamed rice-lentil cake)3 pieces (~120g)110–140 kcal
Sambar (lentil-vegetable stew)1 cup (~150g)90–130 kcal
Coconut chutney2 tbsp60–90 kcal
Total~250–350 kcal

Idli itself is about as calorie-predictable as food gets — fermented rice-and-lentil batter, steamed with no added fat. Sambar's calorie count depends mostly on how many vegetables and how much lentil (vs. thin broth) it contains, plus a small tempering of oil or ghee. The chutney, made from ground coconut, carries more fat per spoonful than either the idli or sambar, so portion size there matters more than people expect.

Why idli sambar is one of the "easier" meals to estimate

Compared to a curry or a fried snack, idli sambar has relatively little hidden variability. Idli's steaming process means there's no oil to miss. Sambar's tempering is small and consistent across most kitchens. The main variable is really the chutney — a generous, oil-heavy tempering on top of the coconut base, versus a light one — and even that swing is smaller than the 100+ calorie gaps seen in richer dishes like butter chicken or biryani.

That said, portion count still matters: 3 idlis versus 5, or a generous ladle of sambar versus a light one, changes the total meaningfully even though the food itself is easy to identify. Frequently asked questions

Is idli sambar good for weight loss?

Yes, it's one of the better options among common Indian breakfasts for weight management — idli is steamed rather than fried, and sambar adds lentil protein and fiber with only a small amount of tempering oil.

How many calories in coconut chutney?

A typical 2-tablespoon serving of coconut chutney is about 60–90 calories, mostly from the coconut's natural fat, with a small addition from the tempering oil.

Is idli healthier than dosa?

Generally yes — idli is steamed with no added oil, while dosa is pan-cooked with oil or ghee on the tawa, which adds calories even to a plain dosa before any filling is considered.
