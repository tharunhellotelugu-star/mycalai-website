How many calories are in dal tadka?

A standard bowl of dal tadka (about 200g, roughly 1 cup) runs 180–260 calories, depending on how it's tempered. That range is wide on purpose — dal tadka's calorie count is driven far more by the tadka (the ghee-and-spice tempering poured on top) than by the lentils themselves, and that tempering varies a lot between a home kitchen and a restaurant.

The base lentils vs. the tempering

Plain boiled dal (just lentils, turmeric, and salt) is genuinely light — about 100–120 calories per cup, mostly protein and fiber with almost no fat. The "tadka" part is where most of the calories show up:

ComponentTypical amountCalories added
Plain boiled lentils (1 cup)—100–120 kcal
Ghee or oil tempering1–2 tsp45–90 kcal
Tomato, onion, spicessmall amount10–20 kcal
Total (home-style)180–230 kcal

Restaurant dal tadka almost always uses more ghee than a home kitchen would — it's what makes the tadka smell and taste the way it does, and it's genuinely hard to judge from a photo alone. A glossy sheen or visible oil pooling at the edge of the bowl is usually the biggest single clue that a serving is on the higher end of that range.

Dal is one of the better plant-protein-to-calorie ratios in a typical Indian thali, which is why it holds up well even with a generous tempering.

Dal tadka vs. dal fry vs. plain dal

These three get confused constantly:

  • Plain dal (no tadka): ~100–130 kcal per cup. Lowest fat, still solid protein and fiber.
  • Dal tadka: ~180–260 kcal per cup. Tempering added on top just before serving, usually cumin/mustard seed, garlic, dried red chili, and ghee or oil.
  • Dal fry: ~200–280 kcal per cup. Similar to tadka but the onion-tomato-spice base is usually sautéed into the dal itself rather than poured on top, so it can carry slightly more oil overall depending on preparation.

If you're trying to keep a dal dish lighter without giving up the flavor, asking for it "dry tempered" or with oil instead of ghee, or simply spooning off the visible pool of tempering before eating, makes a real difference — often 40–60 calories per serving.

Why photo-based calorie trackers get this wrong

Most AI food-scanning apps look at a photo of dal tadka and estimate calories from the visible lentils, which is exactly the ingredient that contributes the least variability. The tempering — the part that actually swings the calorie count by 50–100 calories — is often just a faint sheen on the surface, easy for both humans and AI to underestimate.

This is a known limitation of photo-based nutrition scanning in general: hidden or lightly-visible added fat is the single biggest source of underestimation across the category, not just for dal. Frequently asked questions

Is dal tadka healthy?

Yes, in most everyday contexts — it's high in plant protein and fiber, and the fat comes from a small amount of ghee or oil rather than deep frying. The main thing to watch is portion size of the tempering itself if you're managing calorie intake closely.

How many calories in dal tadka with rice?

A typical serving of dal tadka (200g) with a cup of steamed rice adds roughly 180–200 calories from the rice, bringing the combined total to about 360–460 calories.

Is dal tadka good for weight loss?

It can fit well into a weight-loss plan because of its protein and fiber content, which supports fullness — just be mindful of the tempering amount, since that's where the calorie density concentrates.
