Calories in chicken biryani

A standard plate of chicken biryani (about 350g) comes to roughly 450–650 calories, and unlike most curries, the biggest swing factor isn't a sauce — it's how much ghee or oil is layered through the rice itself during cooking.

The rice is doing more calorie work than most people assume — biryani rice isn't just steamed, it's typically layered with ghee and cooked in the fat-infused stock from the meat (the "dum" method), which means the grains themselves absorb a meaningful amount of fat, not just what's visibly drizzled on top.

Biryani styles compared

  • Hyderabadi (kacchi/dum-style): Tends to be richer, since raw marinated meat and rice are layered and cooked together in a sealed pot with generous ghee.
  • Lucknowi (awadhi): Often lighter on spice and oil, with a more delicate, less ghee-heavy rice layer.
  • Restaurant "quick" biryani: Frequently the richest option — extra oil is used to compensate for shorter cooking times and mask a less developed flavor base.

Because these styles look broadly similar on a plate, the calorie difference between a lighter Lucknowi-style biryani and a rich restaurant version can be 150+ calories without an obvious visual tell.

Why biryani is a genuinely hard dish to estimate

Biryani combines two of the harder problems in food-photo calorie estimation at once: portion volume (rice is dense and easy to under- or over-estimate by eye) and hidden fat (the ghee is mixed through the grains, not pooled visibly). A photo of two biryani plates that look nearly identical in size and color can differ by well over 100 calories.

Frequently asked questions

Is chicken biryani good for weight loss?

It can fit into a calorie budget if the portion is controlled and the rice-to-oil ratio is reasonable, but restaurant biryani is often calorie-dense enough that a smaller plate or sharing a portion is the easiest way to manage it.

Is chicken biryani healthier than mutton biryani?

Generally yes — chicken is leaner than mutton, so a chicken biryani typically runs somewhat lower in calories and saturated fat for a similar portion size, though the rice and oil content usually matters more than the protein choice.

Why does biryani vary so much in calories between restaurants?

Biryani's calorie count is driven mainly by how much ghee or oil is layered through the rice during the dum (slow-cooking) process, which varies significantly by kitchen and regional style.
