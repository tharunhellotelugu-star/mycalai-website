Calories in masala dosa

A standard masala dosa (one large crepe with spiced potato filling) comes to roughly 350–450 calories, with the oil used to crisp the dosa on the tawa contributing nearly as much as the potato filling itself.

Breaking down the dish

Potato masala filling~100g110–150 kcal
Accompanying sambar and chutney (small servings)—90–130 kcal
Total~350–450 kcal

The batter itself — fermented rice and urad dal — isn't especially calorie-dense; it's the oil or ghee brushed on the tawa to help the dosa crisp and release cleanly that adds up, especially at restaurants where a generous drizzle is used for that signature crispy-edge texture. The potato filling adds its own moderate calorie load, tempered with a small amount of oil, mustard seeds, and curry leaves.

Masala dosa vs. plain dosa vs. idli

  • Plain dosa (no filling): ~120–180 kcal — same crepe, same tawa oil, without the potato masala.
  • Masala dosa: ~350–450 kcal including typical sides — the potato filling and larger overall portion push this well above plain dosa.
  • Idli (3 pieces): ~110–140 kcal — steamed rather than tawa-cooked, so no added oil in the base itself, making it the lightest of the three.

If you're comparing South Indian breakfast options by calorie density, idli is consistently the lightest, plain dosa sits in the middle, and masala dosa is the heaviest mainly because of its size and filling rather than any single ingredient being unusually calorie-dense.

Why the tawa oil is easy to miss

A well-made dosa looks crisp and golden whether it was cooked with a light oil brushing or a generous one — the visual cue people usually look for (crispiness) doesn't reliably indicate how much oil was actually used, since a skilled cook can get a crisp texture with either amount. This makes dosa a dish where preparation style matters more than what's visually obvious in a photo.

MyCal AI accounts for typical tawa-cooking oil when identifying a dosa, and separately estimates the potato filling's portion and oil content, rather than treating the whole dish as one flat estimate — so a large restaurant-style masala dosa isn't scored the same as a smaller, lighter home-style one.

Frequently asked questions

Is masala dosa healthy?

It can be part of a balanced diet — the fermented batter and potato filling offer some protein and fiber — but the oil used to crisp the dosa on the tawa adds up, especially at restaurants that use it generously for texture.

Is plain dosa lower in calories than masala dosa?

Yes — a plain dosa is typically 120–180 calories, since it skips the potato filling, which adds roughly 90–150 calories on its own depending on how much oil the filling is tempered with.

How many calories in a paper dosa vs. a regular masala dosa?

A paper dosa is thinner and larger in surface area, so despite looking bigger, it often has a similar or only slightly higher calorie count than a regular masala dosa, since the batter itself is spread thinner.
